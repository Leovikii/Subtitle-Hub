# Source evidence and translation review

## Evidence hierarchy by dimension

### SH-SRC-003 — Timing evidence

Prefer: target-video embedded timing that has been sampled and found useful; actual target audio/waveform/shots; external subtitles proven to match the exact cut; other releases for manual comparison only. Same-container timing is strong provenance, not immunity from errors.

### SH-SRC-004 — Source-text evidence

Prefer: official same-language subtitles/scripts matching the audio; same-language text confirmed by listening; multiple independent transcriptions; translated subtitles only as aids. Official captions may be hearing-impaired, censored, condensed, or from another cut, so resolve conflicts against actual audio and record the decision.

### SH-SRC-005 — Chinese evidence

Every existing Chinese subtitle is a translation candidate, never source-language evidence. Confirmed terminology and project decisions constrain expression but cannot override actual source meaning.

## Translation objective

### SH-TRANS-002 — Accuracy, clarity, voice

Apply in this order:

1. Preserve speaker, object, action, negation, number, tense/aspect, causality, qualification, information order, and attitude.
2. Make modern Simplified Chinese understandable in one viewing.
3. Preserve register, rhythm, humor, and character voice without adding unsupported emotion, logic, or lore.

Review at least: mishearing; mistranslation; omission; addition/overtranslation; undertranslation; register; terminology; continuity; Chinese grammar; punctuation; segmentation; and timing. Treat negation, conditionals, numbers, names, object relations, military/technical actions, jokes, and reveals as high risk.

## Evidence-led correction

### SH-TRANS-003 — Build a reproducible chain

For high-risk edits, record a chain such as target timestamp → actual audio → source subtitle/script → context or image → Chinese change. If evidence is insufficient, keep the most neutral defensible wording, open an issue, and state what evidence would close it. Do not guess or choose by frequency among translations.

### SH-TRANS-004 — Reactions and unsupported particles

Chinese-baseline-only “啊/嗯/哎/哦”, laughter, sighs, and other reactions do not prove that the audio contains them. When reliable source text and other independent target-video evidence omit them, treat them as addition/overtranslation candidates and check the actual audio. Delete only after confirming no meaningful utterance; retain/retranslate only when the audio supports it and align it to the real boundary.

Even source particles such as `えっ`, `あっ`, `うむ`, or `まあ` are not mechanically transliterated. Use audio, context, speaker intent, and punctuation function. Auxiliary translations may help but cannot override the source language.

### SH-TRANS-005 — Compression limits

Compress only when reading/visual constraints genuinely require it. Prefer removing oral redundancy while preserving negation and qualifications, names/addressees, quantities/direction/order/causality, technical terms and plot words, register and offensiveness, jokes, foreshadowing, and reveal order. Never reveal information before its audio or image.

### SH-TRANS-006 — Secondary-language handling

Use reliable text for the secondary language and pair it to the corresponding Chinese event. It does not become an intermediary authority for Chinese. Its spelling and punctuation follow that language, while timing and visual layout follow the Chinese-primary system.
